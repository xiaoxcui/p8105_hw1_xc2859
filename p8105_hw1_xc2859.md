**Create a data frame**

``` r
set.seed(1)

data_df = tibble(

    c("level 1", "level 2", "level 3",
```

The created data frame contains a random sample of size 10 from a
standard Normal distribution (`norm_samp`), a logical vector indicating
whether elements of the sample are greater than 0 (`logical_vec`), a
character vector of length 10 (`character_vec`), and a factor vector of
length 10 (`factor_vec`), with 3 different factor “levels” (level 1,
level 2, and level 3).

**Take the mean of each variable**

``` r
mean(pull(data_df, norm_samp))
```

The mean can be calculated for only 2 variables, `norm_samp` and
`logical_vec`. `norm_samp` is a numeric variable and we can naturally
get its mean. `logical_vec` is a logical variable, and R treats the
`TRUE` result as 1 and `FALSE` result as 0, so its mean represents the
proportion of observations that are `TRUE`. Therefore, we can calculate
the means for these two variables.

However, we cannot get the means of `character_vec` and `factor_vec`
because they are not numeric or logical variables.

**Convert variables to numeric**

``` r
as.numeric(pull(data_df, logical_vec))
as.numeric(pull(data_df, character_vec))
as.numeric(pull(data_df, factor_vec)) 
```

After applying `as.numeric`, the logical variable is converted to 1 for
`TRUE` and 0 for `FALSE`. The character variable can be converted to
numeric if its values are numbers. But in this example, `character_vec`
cannot be successfully coerced to numeric because R cannot interpret the
content of this variable as numbers, so it shows as `NA`. The factor
variable is converted to the integer codes corresponding to its factor
levels.

This helps explain why the mean can be calculated for logical variable,
since logical values can be treated as 1 and 0 by R directly. Character
variables, however, cannot be converted to meaningful numeric values in
all cases. Although a factor can be converted to integer codes, these
codes are the factor levels, and taking their means would not be
meaningful.
